Beerformatics is back next Thursday to Leuven!

We are excited to welcome three speakers who will present their latest research spanning cancer biology, spatial transcriptomics, and neurodegeneration.

[![full](/images/poster/Beerformatics_speakers.png)](/images/poster/Beerformatics_speakers.png)

### **Yura Song** - _𝗜𝗻𝗻𝗮𝘁𝗲 𝗶𝗺𝗺𝘂𝗻𝗶𝘁𝘆 𝗱𝗿𝗶𝘃𝗲𝘀 𝗲𝗽𝗶𝘁𝗵𝗲𝗹𝗶𝗮𝗹 𝗰𝗲𝗹𝗹 𝗽𝗹𝗮𝘀𝘁𝗶𝗰𝗶𝘁𝘆 𝗱𝘂𝗿𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗽𝗿𝗼𝘀𝘁𝗮𝘁𝗲 𝘁𝘂𝗺𝗼𝗿 𝗳𝗼𝗿𝗺𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻_

Yura will present work investigating the early stages of prostate cancer initiation. Deletion of Pten in epithelial basal cells in mice triggers cell plasticity, lineage infidelity, and tumour formation, orchestrated by innate immunity activation and NF-κB signalling in prostate basal stem cells. The findings provide insights with implications for human prostate cancer.

Yura is a computational biologist in the laboratory of Cédric Blanpain at Université libre de Bruxelles, studying molecular mechanisms and gene regulatory networks that regulate epithelial lineage plasticity in mammary and prostate tissues.

### **Geethika Arekatla** - _𝗦𝗽𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗮𝗹 𝘁𝗿𝗮𝗻𝘀𝗰𝗿𝗶𝗽𝘁𝗼𝗺𝗶𝗰𝘀 𝘁𝗼 𝘂𝗻𝗱𝗲𝗿𝘀𝘁𝗮𝗻𝗱 𝗱𝗶𝘀𝗲𝗮𝘀𝗲 𝗺𝗲𝗰𝗵𝗮𝗻𝗶𝘀𝗺𝘀 𝘂𝗻𝗱𝗲𝗿𝗹𝘆𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗔𝗟𝗦_

Geethika will discuss how spatial transcriptomics can uncover disease mechanisms in ALS, a fatal neurodegenerative disease characterized by motor neuron loss in the spinal cord. Because motor neurons represent only about 1% of all cells, they are difficult to capture with dissociative omics approaches. Spatial transcriptomics enables their characterization directly within tissue context.

Geethika is a postdoctoral researcher in the lab of Neurobiology at VIB-KU Leuven Center For Neuroscience, working with multi-omics data to understand neurodegeneration. She previously completed her PhD in Systems Biology at ETH Zurich.

### **Julia Bandera** - _𝗦𝘁𝗿𝘂𝗰𝘁𝘂𝗿𝗮𝗹 𝗱𝗲𝘁𝗲𝗿𝗺𝗶𝗻𝗮𝗻𝘁𝘀 𝗼𝗳 γ-𝘀𝗲𝗰𝗿𝗲𝘁𝗮𝘀𝗲 𝗳𝘂𝗻𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻 𝗮𝗻𝗱 𝗱𝘆𝘀𝗳𝘂𝗻𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻 𝗶𝗻 𝗙𝗮𝗺𝗶𝗹𝗶𝗮𝗹 𝗔𝗹𝘇𝗵𝗲𝗶𝗺𝗲𝗿’𝘀 𝗗𝗶𝘀𝗲𝗮𝘀𝗲_

Julia studies γ-secretase, an intramembrane protease responsible for cleaving the amyloid precursor protein into amyloid-β peptides. Mutations in this complex shift production toward longer fragments that form amyloid plaques, a hallmark of Alzheimer’s disease. Her work combines computational and experimental approaches to investigate the structural and molecular mechanisms underlying γ-secretase dysfunction.

Julia completed a Master’s in Bioinformatics at KU Leuven and is currently a PhD student in the Chávez-Gutiérrez lab at VIB-KU Leuven Center For Neuroscience.
