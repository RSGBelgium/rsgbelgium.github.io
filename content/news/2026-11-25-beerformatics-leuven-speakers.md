## Maria Georgopoulou: _Can Healthy Cells Help Us Understand Brain Disorders?_

Maria's talk explores how natural variation in healthy oligodendrocytes, the cells responsible for producing myelin, could provide valuable insights into how these cells respond to neurological diseases.

Maria is a medical doctor and postdoctoral researcher in computational neurobiology, working in the laboratories of Philip Van Damme (Laboratory of Neurobiology) and Alejandro Sifrim (Laboratory of Multi-omic Integrative Bioinformatics) at KU Leuven.

## Eren Can Ekşi: _Discovery of Genomic Regulatory Principles in a Whole Organism Through Sequence-to-Function Models_

Eren's talk explores how sequence-to-function models trained on two whole-organism single-cell atlases of the fruit fly can uncover regulatory principles beyond those identified through individual tissues or cell lines.

Eren is a PhD researcher in the Laboratory of Computational Biology at VIB.AI and KU Leuven, supervised by Professor Stein Aerts. With a background in Molecular Biology and Genetics, his research investigates gene regulation using computational approaches.

## Robert Forsyth: _Long-Read Sequencing for Precision Sarcoma Diagnostics_

Robert's talk addresses the diagnostic challenges of sarcomas, a highly heterogeneous group of cancers with over 100 subtypes. He will discuss how long-read sequencing combined with Fiber-Seq could streamline molecular diagnostics and reveal functional genomic variation that conventional approaches may miss.

Robert is a third-year PhD student in Biomedical Sciences at KU Leuven, originally from the USA. He works in the Demeulemeester Lab at the VIB-KU Leuven Center for Cancer Biology, and his research focuses on applying long-read sequencing to understand complex genomes, particularly in cancer.

## Katerina Rapti: _Mapping Enhancer–Promoter Contacts in Human Craniofacial Development_

Katerina will present promoter interaction maps generated across different cell types during human craniofacial development, demonstrating how these maps can connect candidate enhancers to target genes and support the interpretation of non-coding variants identified in patients.

Katerina is a medical doctor and third-year PhD researcher at the Laboratory of Cytogenetics and Genome Research, KU Leuven. Her research focuses on identifying non-coding genetic variants that alter gene regulation and contribute to craniofacial malformations.
